# AI-PhD.SKILL: Should I Write a Rebuttal?

[← Index](../../README.md) · [中文原文](0.2-Should-I-Rebuttal.md) · English

Whenever conference reviews come out, discussion threads fill with questions like these, using a 1–6 scoring scale:

- “My scores are 2, 3, 3, 4. Should I withdraw?”
- “Is a rebuttal worth writing for 3, 3, 4, 4?”
- “What do I do with 2, 2, 5, 5?”

The absolute scores are often less important than your paper's relative position in the area chair's batch. The AC makes the recommendation; the numbers do not make it by themselves.

Initial scores also tell you only so much. Many reviewers are quite willing to raise their scores during rebuttal. For example, if a paper is good overall but hard to follow on a first read, with several points I do not understand, an effective clarification can make me move from 3 to 5, or even 6. I may also raise my score after rebuttal for a strong idea with weaknesses in the writing.

So look at the reviewer's **attitude**, not just the score. You can often infer it from the comments:

- The best case is an **explicit** statement such as “I would raise my score if …”
- Another promising case is a reviewer who is clearly confused and **wants** specific points explained. It is common for reviewers to remain confused about something already written in the paper. That still gives you room to help through rebuttal.
- A harder case is a reviewer who asks only minor, peripheral questions and then goes silent. There may be little you can do. Luck plays a meaningful role in research; repeated submissions can at least help reduce its variance.

Here are a few tentative lessons from my own experience:

- A rebuttal is written for the AC as well as the reviewers.
- Try to earn recognition without accommodating every request uncritically.
- Do not let reviewers pull the discussion away from your main contribution.
- Be concise and address the core issues. Everyone has limited time, and the reviewing workload at ICML this year has been especially heavy.

Good luck with your rebuttals.
