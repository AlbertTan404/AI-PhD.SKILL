# AI-PhD.SKILL: Reaching Out

[← Index](../../README.md) · [中文原文](2-Reach-Out.md) · English

When I first started my PhD, paper authors, professors, and industry experts all felt distant. I mostly spoke with my own advisor and labmates.

Two things gradually changed. I began to see that **the world is open to interaction**, and that **taking the initiative to connect has positive expected value**.

Many connections will not happen unless you initiate them.

Here are three experiences.

## 1. Asking about a paper

During my first PhD year, I read a paper that interested me, but several important details remained unclear. Eventually, I worked up the courage to email the first author.

They sent a very detailed reply, including implementation details that the paper had not covered.

That exchange directly helped my subsequent experiments.

It taught me something simple: a paper does not contain everything its authors know. A thoughtful question can give you access to more of that knowledge.

## 2. Connecting related research

While working at MSRA in 2023, we and other students produced several research projects.

Later, we came across a VLA paper from a large group whose central idea substantially overlapped with an early version of our work.

A labmate emailed the first author, explained the related work, and invited them to discuss and compare it in their second arXiv version.

They soon added a citation and discussion in the updated paper.

That was the first time I clearly saw that a connection between research projects could be established through direct communication, without depending entirely on the submission process.

## 3. At CVPR

CVPR was in Denver this year, during the period when I was preparing for autumn recruiting.

I applied for several dinners and invitation-only events, but initially did not receive an invitation to the Tencent Qingyun dinner.

The day before the event, I went to the booth to ask about it.

The staff said the event was full and that attending was generally impossible without an invitation email.

I explained my recruiting situation, why I wanted to attend, and that I hoped to make one more attempt.

A representative then spoke with me individually, briefly looked over my background, felt it was a good fit, and helped add me to the list and move the approval forward.

Shortly after I left the booth, I received a message saying I could collect a ticket.

It brought home the feeling that **the world is open to interaction**.

I was also asked not to publicize the additional invitation route, and I have been careful about that.

The experience became one of the highlights of the conference for me.

---

All three experiences point to the same idea: research and professional networks are systems you can actively engage with.

There are boundaries, of course. My main principles are:

1. **Be sincere.** Explain your motivation clearly and avoid overpackaging yourself.
2. **Minimize the burden on others.** Respect their time and improve the quality of your questions.
3. **Accept rejection.** Taking the initiative may have positive expected value without guaranteeing success on any particular attempt.
4. **Do not assume entitlement.** Help is something the other person chooses to give.

---

Looking back, what matters most is the change in perspective, beyond the individual outcomes.

Many connections can be actively built. Learning to do that can have a growing impact throughout a PhD.
